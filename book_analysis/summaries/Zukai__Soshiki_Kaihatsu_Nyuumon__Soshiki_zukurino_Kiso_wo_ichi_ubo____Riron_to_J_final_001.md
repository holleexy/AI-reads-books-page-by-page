## Fundamentals of Organizations

### What is an Organization?
- **Definition**: An organization is not merely a "Group" (集団). It is a collective with clear requirements, defined by shared purposes and collaborative effort (協働).
- **Key Definitions**:
  - *Edgar Schein*: Rational collaboration through division of labor and hierarchy to achieve a common goal.
  - *Chester Barnard*: A system of consciously coordinated human activities.
- **Barnard's 3 Conditions for an Organization**:
  1. Common organizational purpose
  2. Willingness to contribute and collaborate
  3. Smooth communication
- **Organizational Effectiveness**: True effectiveness is the dual achievement of the organization's shared purpose and the individual purposes of its members.

### The Evolution of Organizational Development (OD)
- **Origins**: Originated in the 1940s in the U.S., introduced to Japan in the 1950s, fell out of favor, and regained prominence around 2000 alongside coaching and facilitation.
- **Modern Context**: Driven by workplace diversity. Traditional homogeneous management (single management) has failed, causing systemic distortions and individual pain.
- **Hard vs. Soft Aspects**: Building systems (Hard) is insufficient; vitality and culture (Soft) must flow through them. 
  > "Creating a Buddha but not putting the soul in" (仏作って魂入れず) – Form without substance is worse than no form at all.
- **Definition of "Development"**: *Development* means growth or maturation, not external creation. The true protagonists of OD are the organization's members; external consultants are merely supporters.
- **Warwick's Definition**: OD is a planned, collaborative process to enhance an organization's health, effectiveness, and self-renovation capacity.
- **Four Interventions (Cummings & Worley)**:
  1. **Human Process Interventions**: (Most crucial) Directly addressing interpersonal relationships.
  2. **Techno-structural Interventions**: Work design/structure.
  3. **Human Resource Management Interventions**: HR systems, development.
  4. **Strategic Change Interventions**: Vision, strategy, mergers.
- **The "Gap" in Japanese Companies**: Most Japanese org charts lack a specific department for "Human Process Interventions" and departmental-level strategy, leaving a functional gap.

## Philosophical Roots and History of OD

### The Three Philosophical Roots (1900s)
1. **John Dewey (Pragmatism)**: Learning through reflection on experience. Moving from passive to active human engagement.
2. **Edmund Husserl (Phenomenology)**: Focus on the "here & now" and subjective consciousness.
3. **Sigmund Freud (Psychoanalysis)**: Bringing the "unconscious" (hidden psychological conflicts) to the surface through dialogue.

### Foundational Methods and Theories (1920s)
1. **Group Psychotherapy**: Using group dynamics to treat trauma (e.g., Jacob Moreno's Psychodrama, Fritz Perls' Gestalt Therapy).
2. **Human Relations Theory**: George Elton Mayo's Hawthorne Experiments shifted focus from scientific management (treating humans as machines) to human emotions and relationships.
3. **Barnard's Organizational Theory**: Viewing the organization as a "System" (General Systems Theory).

### The Birth of OD (1940s)
- **Kurt Lewin & T-Groups**: The origin of OD. Lewin, a refugee from Nazi Germany, valued democratic, non-discriminatory processes. T-Groups (Training Groups) focused on "here & now" interpersonal dynamics.
- **NTL (National Training Laboratories)**: Founded in 1947, became the headquarters for OD pioneers (McGregor, Argyris, Schein).
- **Rensis Likert & Survey Feedback**: Developed the "Survey -> Feedback -> Dialogue" sequence, laying the foundation for the **OD Map** and two-dimensional leadership (Task vs. Relationship).

### OD in Japan
1. **Sensitivity Training (ST)**: Introduced from the US, it became a short-lived boom. Failed due to a lack of qualified trainers, leading to dangerous psychological outcomes.
2. **QC Circles**: Quality Control circles focused on shop-floor autonomy, mutual development, and continuous improvement. Later adopted by the US into OD practices.

### Evolution and Paradigm Shifts
- **1960s (Best Way Era)**: Driven by the human rights movement. Believed in a singular ideal organization (e.g., Likert's System 4, Blake & Mouton's Managerial Grid).
- **1970s (Contingency Era)**: Shifted to "strategy dictates structure" (Chandler). Focus on adapting to external environments.
- **1990s (Winter of OD)**: Declined due to the rise of top-down, results-driven "Change Management" by large consulting firms.
- **2000s (Resurgence)**: Dialogic OD emerged, led by David Cooperrider's **Appreciative Inquiry (AI)**, shifting focus from problems to strengths.
- **Ken Wilber's Integral Theory**: Categorizes history into four quadrants: Interior/Exterior vs. Individual/Collective (I/We/It). OD fundamentally resides in the "We" (Interior-Collective / Goodness) domain.

## The OD Practitioner

- **Change Agents**: Practitioners must base their work on 4 core values from the NTL Handbook:
  1. **Human Respect** (Y-Theory).
  2. **Democratic** (Participative decision-making).
  3. **Client-Centered** (The client owns the change).
  4. **Environmental/Social Orientation** (Organic system coexisting with society).
- **Use of Self**: The primary tool for OD is the practitioner's own values, not HR systems.
- **Process Consultation (Edgar Schein)**: The philosophy of helping. Practitioners must flexibly switch between:
  1. *Expert Model*: Providing solutions.
  2. *Doctor-Patient Model*: Diagnosing and prescribing.
  3. *Process Consultation Model*: Co-creating understanding and solutions with the client.
- **Risk of Malpractice**: Group psychology tools can be dangerous (e.g., the ST tragedies in Japan). Practitioners must prioritize *Care* and *Meaning-making* over forced emotional stimulation or execution.

## The OD Map: Survey Feedback

### Principles and Mechanisms
- **Definition**: A diagnostic OD method starting with Rensis Likert. It visualizes organizational state via data to catalyze dialogue and action.
- **HR Tech Era**: While surveys are now easy and cheap, data collection alone changes nothing ("Weighing yourself doesn't make you lose weight").
- **Three Theoretical Effects**:
  1. **Collection Effect**: The act of asking questions itself changes behavior (sends a message).
  2. **Feedback Effect**: Discrepancies between data and self-image create motivation (Motivation) and direction (Direction) for change.
  3. **Externalization Effect**: Separates the problem from the individual, allowing objective dialogue without personal attacks ("Saying the unspoken").

### The 8-Step OD Map
1. **Entry & Contracting**: Agreeing on the psychological contract (roles, goals, but not specific actions).
2. **Data Collection**: Surveys, 360-degree feedback.
3. **Data Analysis**: Simple tabulation (Means and Distributions). *Do not overcomplicate.*
4. **Feedback**: Presenting data simply to the frontline.
5. **Action Planning**: Collaborative planning.
6. **Action Implementation**: Client takes the lead.
7. **Evaluation**: Measuring effectiveness.
8. **Termination / Loop**: Closing or returning to Step 2.

### Survey Design and Pitfalls
- **4 Failure Patterns**: 1) Expecting too much from data, 2) Leaving results unshared, 3) Self-serving interpretations (being lenient on oneself), 4) Obsessing over scores (metric fixation).
- **Design**: Questions must be reverse-engineered from the desired organizational state.
- **David Nadler's 7 Principles**: Relevant, actionable, concise, quick, understandable, reliable, benchmarkable.

### Feedback Meeting Facilitation
- **The Owner**: The person with ultimate responsibility for the team (e.g., CEO for company, Manager for team). Without the Owner's genuine commitment, the meeting fails.
- **Facilitator**: Needs influence and the skill to positively promote dialogue.
- **Steps**: Purpose explanation -> Ground rules -> Data presentation -> Interpretation -> Future dialogue -> Action planning -> Follow-up.
- **Nudge Theory**: Use "Good & More" sheets (praise first, then improve) to create psychological safety.

## Dialogic Organizational Development

### Dialogue vs. Discussion
- **Discussion**: Analytical, aiming to decide *HOW* for a predetermined *WHY*. (Battle of opinions).
- **Dialogue**: Exploratory, aiming to co-create the *WHY* (Common Good) by sharing premises and contexts.
- **Modern Necessity**: Remote work and diversity have destroyed the shared "unconscious premises" that once existed in homogeneous teams. Dialogue must now be intentional.
- **Social Constructionism**: Reality is not objectively observed but socially constructed through human perception and agreement ("Words create worlds").

### Changing Mindsets
- **Diagnostic vs. Dialogic**: Coined by Bushe & Marshak (2009). Diagnostic relies on positivism (finding the objective truth/problem). Dialogic relies on social constructionism (co-creating reality through relationships).
- **Success Cycle Model (Daniel Kim)**: High *Quality of Relationships* -> High *Quality of Thinking* -> High *Quality of Action* -> High *Quality of Results*.
- **Psychological Safety (Amy Edmondson)**: Not just being "nice," but an environment of high learning and innovation where one can speak up, disagree constructively, and take risks.

### Whole System Approaches
1. **Appreciative Inquiry (AI)**: 4D Cycle (Discovery, Dream, Design, Destiny). Focuses on strengths (Positive Core) rather than problems.
2. **Future Search**: Gathering diverse stakeholders to map the past/present and co-create a future action plan.
3. **Open Space Technology (OST)**: Self-organized discussions based on the "Law of Two Feet" (move where you are learning/contributing).
4. **World Café**: Relaxed, rotating small-group dialogues to cross-pollinate ideas.

## The Learning Organization

### Peter Senge's Fifth Discipline
- **Core Concept**: An organization that continuously learns and adapts by overcoming "Learning Disabilities" (e.g., "I am my position," "The enemy is out there," "Boiled frog syndrome").
- **The 5 Disciplines**:
  1. **Personal Mastery**: Clarifying personal vision and holding *Creative Tension* (the gap between vision and reality).
  2. **Mental Models**: Uncovering deeply ingrained assumptions. Utilizing the "Ladder of Inference" and balancing Inquiry and Advocacy.
  3. **Shared Vision**: Built from individual visions, not top-down compliance.
  4. **Team Learning**: Mastering *Dialogue* (exploring together) and *Discussion* (analyzing/deciding).
  5. **Systems Thinking**: The 5th discipline. Seeing interrelationships and processes (loops) rather than linear snapshots. Understanding "Reinforcing" and "Balancing" loops and "Delays."

### Leadership in a Learning Organization
- Leaders are *Designers*, *Stewards*, and *Teachers*.
- They build the "Clock" (the enduring organization) rather than just telling the "Time" (a single great product/idea).
- **Robert Kegan's Contribution**: Introduced adult cognitive development stages. Recognized that organizations cannot change if individual consciousness levels (subjectivity) do not evolve.

### The U Theory (Otto Scharmer)
- A methodology for sensing and actualizing future possibilities, heavily influenced by Eastern/Buddhist philosophy, moving away from purely analytical system thinking.

## Teal Organizations & Integral Theory

### Integral Theory (Ken Wilber)
- **A Theory of Everything**: A framework integrating all aspects of reality (Truth, Goodness, Beauty) via Four Quadrants (Interior/Exterior x Individual/Collective).
- **Spiral Dynamics**: Developmental stages of human consciousness (Colors: Red, Amber, Orange, Green, Teal).
- **Principles**: "Transcend and Include." Higher stages embrace lower stages. Growth hierarchies (Holon/Kernelarchy) are natural and healthy, unlike rigid dominator hierarchies.
- **Transformation Process**: Achieving -> Dissonance -> Insight -> Opening.

### Teal Organizations (Frederic Laloux)
- **Definition**: Evolutionary organizations characterized by:
  1. **Self-Management**: No traditional hierarchy/bosses.
  2. **Wholeness**: Dropping professional "masks" and bringing the whole self to work.
  3. **Evolutionary Purpose**: The organization has a life and direction of its own, driven by purpose, not just profit.
- **Prerequisites for Teal**: The CEO and Board (Owners) must possess "Teal" consciousness. Without this, the transition fails.
- **Case Study - Zappos**: Successfully achieved Green culture (Delivering Happiness) after founder Tony Hsieh experienced the limits of Orange (achievement) culture. Attempted to transition to Teal via **Holacracy** (a self-management operating system). Offered a "Teal Offer" (severance) to those who disagreed, resulting in an 18% turnover but ultimately a resilient, self-managing structure.

## Psychological Management (Recruit's Model)

### Core Philosophy: "Let individuals be as they are"
- Pioneered by Osawa (Recruit's founding HR executive). Views human management through the lens of psychology, accepting contradictions and conflicts.
- **6 Elements**:
  1. **Motivation**: Fostering Competence, Autonomy (Self-determination), and Recognition.
  2. **Autonomous Teams**: Clear belonging, small size (5-7), strong solidarity, uninterrupted decision-making.
  3. **Organizational Activation**: Intentionally creating chaos to destroy rigid order (Unlearning).
  4. **Leadership**: Defined as *Influence*, not title. Requires "Willingness, Empathy, Communication, and Trust." Measured by the members' perception, not the leader's intent.
  5. **Aptitude**: Using SPI/MBTI for self-understanding and job-fit, assessing across three dimensions: Job adaptation, Workplace adaptation, Self-adaptation.
  6. **Individualization**: Using MBTI (Jungian psychology) to help individuals understand their "Gifts" (personality), fostering self-acceptance and team synergy.

## The Wise Company (Knowledge Creation)

### SECI Model (Ikujiro Nonaka)
- **Knowledge vs. Information**: Knowledge has purpose, context, and requires action/belief.
- **Tacit vs. Explicit**: Knowledge is created through the dynamic conversion between Tacit (subjective/experiential) and Explicit (objective/conceptual).
- **SECI Processes**: Socialization (Tacit-Tacit), Externalization (Tacit-Explicit), Combination (Explicit-Explicit), Internalization (Explicit-Tacit).

### Phronesis (Practical Wisdom)
- **Definition**: Aristotle's concept of practical wisdom. The ability to discern the essence of things and act ethically in the "here and now."
- **6 Practices of Wise Leaders**:
  1. **Judge Goodness**: Determine the common good (e.g., YKK's Cycle of Goodness).
  2. **Grasp Essence**: Using intuition and direct experience (e.g., Soichiro Honda testing bikes himself).
  3. **Create "Ba" (Place)**: Building spaces for mutual interaction and knowledge creation (e.g., Honda's "Waigaya").
  4. **Communicate Essence**: Using Rhetoric (Logos, Pathos, Ethos), Metaphor, and Storytelling.
  5. **Exercise Political Power**: Moving people toward a good purpose using Dialectics and Middle-Up-Down management.
  6. **Foster Practical Wisdom**: Distributing leadership so everyone acts wisely (e.g., Modern apprenticeship, Scrum/Amoeba management, Jazz-like improvisation).
