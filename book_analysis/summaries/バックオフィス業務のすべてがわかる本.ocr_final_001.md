## What Back Office Is—and Why It Is Undervalued

*Back office* (バックオフィス) is the collective term for accounting, HR, general affairs, and legal—also called *management department* (管理部門), *indirect department* (間接部門), or *corporate department* (コーポレート部門). It is contrasted with the *front office* (販売, アフターサービス, 企画・開発, 製造, 物流, マーケティング, サービス).

If management functions themselves become the company’s core, it can be a warning that actual business is not progressing well.

**Common complaints from other departments:**
- “Clerical work anyone can do, no pressure, looks easy”
- “We can’t see what they actually do”
- “They don’t understand the field/business”
- “They only say rigid or conservative things”
- “They generate no sales, only costs”

The key solution is **connection with management** (経営との接続). Highest roles include CFO, CHRO, and increasingly CLO and CAO. CXO-level leaders typically started as individual contributors; the difference is *involvement in management* (経営への関与).

- **CFO mission:** optimize company-wide allocation of financial resources (including M&A)
- **CHRO mission:** build an organization that produces the strongest performance via system design, hiring, and culture

True back-office work is handing over part of the founder/manager’s “company management control.” Essence and true mission: understand the manager’s intent and run operations from the same viewpoint.

### Three Characteristics of Back-Office Work

1. Higher proportion of **routine/standardized** work than non-routine work
2. Work requiring **specialized knowledge** (laws, accounting rules)
3. Work that **governs company-wide management control** (経営管理)

Tasks are frequently performed in daily, weekly, monthly, or yearly cycles. Example: monthly payment processing—receive invoices, compile the payment list, execute transfers, record results.

Required knowledge is at executive level and is not static: laws change every year. The value back office should deliver is supporting management decision-making from **people, goods, and money** (ヒト・モノ・カネ) and guiding the organization toward problem-solving.

Personnel who correctly understand and embody these three characteristics are scarce.

## Digitalization and the Changing Talent Standard

From the early 2010s, digitalization has been an unavoidable transformation. Traditional Japanese companies often positioned back office as standardized clerical processing, with insufficient specialized knowledge and management-control functions. This is being disrupted by **DX** (Digital Transformation)—described as urgent (“no waiting” / 待ったなし).

**Core automation techniques:**
- *RPA* (Robotic Process Automation): automates searching, downloading, inputting, and analyzing data
- *OCR* (Optical Character Recognition) + AI: converts paper into digital text and reduces data-entry labor

Traditional systems were heavily customized and rigid. Modern **cloud** systems emphasize UI/UX and API linkage. Most clerical/transaction-processing functions are being replaced by systems.

**System skills required (not building systems):**
- Understand DX purposes
- Reorganize routine work and judge what can be systematized
- Compare and evaluate commercial systems
- Drive implementation as responsible staff
- Know internal processing and master-data design
- Collaborate with vendors and IT on error handling

```
Input (情報の入力) → internal processing by predetermined rules → Output (処理結果)
```

Understanding a system means knowing what must be entered, what processing occurs, and what results it produces.

### Legal Knowledge Pressure

Back-office work cannot be performed without laws and rules. Relevant statutes include Companies Act, Antimonopoly Act, Subcontract Act, Labor Standards Act, tax laws, accounting standards, Patent Act, Financial Instruments and Exchange Act, Worker Dispatching Act, and many others.

Laws are amended almost every year. In an SNS era, violations cause larger losses. Required actions:
1. Regularly collect law-related information
2. Collaborate with domain experts (lawyers, tax accountants, CPAs, labor consultants, patent attorneys)
3. Evaluate impact of legal/institutional changes
4. Form projects according to impact
5. Involve internal parties and communicate legal information

### Talent Shortage and BPO

Traditional back-office talent had low turnover, long tenure, low liquidity, and historically not high quality or compensation. Rapid environmental change produced a mismatch: companies now need higher skill (including IT literacy) while pay has not kept pace.

**BPO** (Business Process Outsourcing) and licensed specialists (lawyers, patent attorneys, tax accountants, CPAs, labor consultants) can supplement gaps. Accounting and HR/labor are relatively easy to outsource.

**Outsourcing advantages:** cover insufficient internal knowledge; use resources only when needed.

**Disadvantages:** communication costs; finding good partners; drawing boundaries; often more expensive than internal work. Completely handing work over (*丸投げ*) is dangerous. Internal personnel must act as instructors/intermediaries. Neglecting the internal-vs-external design will not produce a functioning system even if money is spent.

## From Clerical Work to Professional Work

Historically, much back office was simple clerical duty (tea service, photocopying, vouchers, mailing)—associated with office women in uniforms. Internal division: clerical staff vs. career-track generalists vs. internally promoted managers.

That distinction is collapsing under DX, legal revisions/compliance, and labor shortages. Accounting clerks who created invoices and bookkept now see those tasks automated. Remaining roles shift toward **professional work, management, and operational design**.

**Ability to design work** (業務を設計する力) is equivalent to *management ability*:
1. What work is done
2. Who performs it
3. When and on what schedule

Visualizing and verbalizing work enables manuals so anyone produces the same results. Because much work is standardized, organizing flows directly drives efficiency.

**Ideal future talent:**
1. Master IT for tasks DX will replace
2. Broad intermediate-level knowledge across functions
3. Utilize internal and external talent and manage the whole

## Functions Mapped to ヒト・モノ・カネ

- **Accounting/finance (経理財務):** *money* (カネ)—ledgers, financial statements, cash-flow forecasts, fundraising
- **HR/labor (人事労務):** *people* (ヒト)—recruitment, development, personnel systems, labor-law compliance, social-insurance procedures
- **Legal (法務):** recognize and control legal risk—*business legal* (contracts) and *corporate legal* (shareholders’ meetings, board, registrations); plus harassment and governance
- **General affairs (総務):** internal environment and events—company events, gifts, office relocation, OA equipment; the unsung hero (*縁の下の力持ち*)
- **Management planning (経営企画):** hands and feet of management—mid-term plans, budgets, M&A, special projects; relatively few routine tasks

### Organization-Design Patterns

Structure is shaped by business model, growth plans, culture, company type (large/SME/startup), and stakeholder demands.

- **Case 1 — One-person back office:** common in SMEs and early startups; breadth high, depth shallow; specialists (税理士, 社労士) supplement
- **Case 2 — People vs. money:** HR/labor vs. accounting; GA, legal, planning aggregated into one of those two
- **Case 3 — Strategic vs. management/support:** planning + HR (org/recruitment) as middle office vs. defensive risk-management unit (accounting, GA, labor, legal)
- **Case 4 — Separate department per function:** typical around 100 employees total and 10–15 back-office people (経理部, 人事部, 総務部, 法務部, 経営企画部)

Organization design **is** culture formation.

### Two-Axis Mapping of Domains

**Ability axis:** *knowledge power* (知識力) vs. *application power* (応用力)
- Accounting is knowledge-heavy (standards, bookkeeping)
- Management planning is application-heavy (combine market, talent, laws into a surface)

**Decision-making axis:** *coordination/communication* vs. *logic*
- HR evaluation/wage rules: consensus and history
- Legal contract negotiation: cause-and-effect and win-win structure

No one is a “superman.” Staff according to company situation. When talent is insufficient, use BPO. Build in step with growth stage; change structure flexibly.

## Back-Office Policy (バックオフィス・ポリシー)

A policy is the compass that unifies the function. Example values: **攻めて守る** (offense and defense), *Versatile*, **ゼロベース思考** (zero-based thinking), **プロフェッショナリズム**.

**Don’ts:** treating routines as inescapable busywork; spectator attitude; refusing change because “last time you said X”; copying without original thought; other-blame excuses; single-channel communication.

**Do’s:** drop routines when structural change matters; ask how a routine can be solved by a mechanism; self-responsibility; any communication channel that solves the problem; consult immediately when unclear.

**Why a separate policy is needed:**
1. Different domain characteristics require one large common goal
2. Goals are hard to set (cost center); a policy supports growth and career
3. Demonstrates presence as a strategically important function (business plans, fundraising, IPO, M&A)

**Design principles:** link to company-wide strategy; combine passion and logic; express unique character in simple language; leaders with deep back-office *and* management experience.

## Periodic Tasks, Schedules, and Closing

Master tasks via **schedule**, **submission/reporting destinations**, and **documents**. Schedules: annual, monthly, or whenever they arise. Destinations: tax offices, labor standards offices, counterparties, shareholders, banks.

Hold regular internal meetings to visualize deadlines. Annual tasks often have legally mandated deadlines (accounting shifts with fiscal year-end; book assumes March close). Busy/idle cycles are forecastable—plan vacations and overtime accordingly.

**Financial closing (決算業務)** is one of the highest-weight tasks. It looks routine but changes yearly (standards, business developments). Completing it produces strong achievement. Still a rite of passage for accountants despite work-style reform and IT.

## Accounting and Finance (経理財務)

Work is “about money,” but almost all company activity involves money—so **classify** it:

- Standalone closing (books, accounting policy, reporting)
- Consolidated closing
- Funds (fundraising, receipts/payments)
- Overall (budget, mid-term plan, performance analysis)
- Field (KPI, investment decisions)
- Tax (filings, tax planning)

**Three minimum understandings:** laws/rules, stakeholders, the business.

Laws: Companies Act (form, organs, closing schedules, capital/dividends); Financial Instruments and Exchange Act (listed disclosure, internal control); accounting standards (titles, treatments); tax law (calculation, officer compensation, expenses).

Financial statements serve Company Law (creditors/investors), FIEL (shareholders), tax law (authorities), and management.

**Stakeholders shape policy.** Small owner firms: few stakeholders, owner-oriented, tax-saving/inheritance vs. growth. Listed firms: sustainable growth + shareholder returns, fair transparent processing, bank communication.

> 「お金の動きとは、ビジネスが進む過程での結果に過ぎない」  
> Money movements are nothing more than the results of business advancing.

Always grasp **facts** (*ファクト*), not documents at face value. Example: a lease invoice (rent, deposit, key money) requires asking contract start, period, free-rent, prepaid vs. current vs. postpaid, refundability of deposit, brokerage fees, interior construction. Understanding the business **equals** communication with the front office.

### Basic vs. Applied Abilities

**Basic:** accuracy to the 1-yen unit; processing speed (software + Excel); honesty (no leaks, politics, fraud, insider use). Bookkeeping is “the language of accounting.” Start with debit/credit (貸借). Japanese Bookkeeping Level 3 (簿記3級) is recommended.

**Misconceptions:** exceptional innate intelligence, ultra-fast calculation, or CPA/tax qualifications are not strictly required. Tools compensate. Fitting transactions into existing frameworks matters more than inventing logic. Diligence and sincerity often matter more than raw intellect.

### Double-Entry Bookkeeping

*Single-entry* is like a household account book (cash in/out). Large investments distort a single year. *Double-entry* adds a medium- to long-term view via the five classifications:

| Element | Side of statements |
|---|---|
| 資産 (Assets) | B/S |
| 負債 (Liabilities) | B/S |
| 資本/純資産 (Equity) | B/S |
| 収益 (Revenue) | P/L |
| 費用 (Expenses) | P/L |

**Debit (借方):** asset ↑, liability ↓, revenue ↓, expense ↑  
**Credit (貸方):** asset ↓, liability ↑, revenue ↑, expense ↓

Examples:
- Bank loan 10M deposited: Dr 普通預金 / Cr 借入金
- Credit sale 1M: Dr 売掛金 / Cr 売上高
- Rent 500k: Dr 賃借料 / Cr 普通預金

### Three Accounting Principles

1. **Accrual basis (発生主義)** vs. cash basis (現金主義)—record when economic events occur, not when cash moves
2. **Matching principle (費用収益対応の原則)**—match expenses to related revenues (COGS, depreciation)
3. **Going concern (継続企業の前提)**—underpins period accounting, receivables/payables, deferrals, depreciation

Revenue uses the **realization principle (実現主義)** (stricter accrual): sales when goods/services are provided *and* consideration is realized (including AR). From April 2021, large/listed companies apply a new revenue-recognition standard based on fulfillment of *performance obligations* (履行義務)—consult audit firms/CPAs.

**Inventory (periodic method):** purchases charged to 仕入; unsold goods deducted at year-end so COGS = purchases − ending inventory. Unsold goods stay as B/S assets.

**Depreciation (減価償却):** capitalize long-lived assets; allocate over useful life. Straight-line: `取得価額 × (1 ÷ 耐用年数)`. Declining-balance: `簿価 × 償却率` (200% method), then *revised depreciation* when expense falls below the guaranteed amount. Buildings and intangibles must use straight-line; others default to declining-balance unless elected otherwise.

**SME vs. large-enterprise capitalization:**
- SME: under 100k expense; 100k–under 300k may expense (annual cap 3M yen); 300k+ capitalize
- Large: 200k+ capitalize
- Amount test is **per item**, not per PO
- Capital vs. revenue expenditure for repairs: under 200k, cyclic ≤3 years, ordinary maintenance → 修繕費; value/life increase → capitalize; safe harbor under 600k or ≤10% of prior year-end cost
- Depreciation starts on **placed-in-service date**, not purchase. Use 建設仮勘定 (CIP) until then; prorate by months in service

### Consumption Tax (消費税)

VAT-style: `納税額 = 仮受消費税 − 仮払消費税`. Bearer is the consumer; taxpayer is the business. Tax-exempt if sales two years prior ≤10M yen (first two years automatically exempt). Simplified taxation if sales two years prior ≤50M yen, using deemed purchase ratios by business type (Type 1 wholesale 90% … Type 6 real estate 40%).

**税込経理** vs. **税抜経理:** profit does not change; most taxable businesses use tax-exclusive. Classify every journal as 課税 / 非課税 / 免税 / 不課税.

**Invoice System (インボイス制度, Oct 2023):** input credit only with a *Qualified Invoice* (適格請求書). Listing requirements include issuer name + registration number, date, description, amounts by rate, tax by rate, recipient name. Non-compliant invoices raise tax payable. Pressure on formerly tax-exempt suppliers to register.

**Relief measures:**
- Transitional input credit on tax-exempt purchases: 80% (to Sep 2026), then 50% (to Sep 2029)
- 2割特例: former exempt businesses may pay 20% of (taxable sales × rate)
- Small-amount exception: purchases under 10,000 yen (sales ≤100M) through Sep 2029
- Return-invoice exemption for discounts/returns under 10,000 yen

Four judgment axes after Invoice System: taxable type × rate (10%/8%/old 8%) × qualified vs. non-qualified × input-credit method (taxable-sales / non-taxable-sales / common). Highly error-prone.
