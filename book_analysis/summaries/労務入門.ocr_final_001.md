## Core Philosophy and Definition of 労務

### Working Definition
*労務* is fulfilling obligations that arise in labor. Obligations exist on both sides:
- **Enterprise**: protect workers under the law
- **Worker**: provide labor and maintain internal order

Hannah Arendt’s three categories of human activity:
- *Labor* (労働) — toil necessary to sustain biological life (should be reduced)
- *Work* (仕事) — creating lasting artifacts (has meaning and pride)
- *Action* (活動) — appearing and communicating with others (spontaneous and enjoyable)

People in companies typically possess all three. *労務* is the HR domain that underpins the most basic — the activity of staying alive.

> 労務は、人が「生きる」ことを支える重要な人事領域である。

Dictionary definitions of *romu* include labor performed for remuneration (Civil Code Art. 623) and administrative affairs related to labor. The author argues the common “paperwork” view is too narrow.

### 労務 vs. 人材マネジメント (Talent Management)
Both are forms of *人事* (HR) sharing the principle 「人を生かして事をなす」 (make people thrive in order to accomplish things), but they differ sharply in practice.

| Aspect | 労務 (Defensive HR) | 人材マネジメント (Offensive HR) |
|--------|---------------------|--------------------------------|
| Equality | Treats every hard-working person equally | Premise of *不平等* (unequal treatment) |
| Method | Protects all so they do not suffer | Preferentially invests in selected talent to win results |
| Focus | Livelihoods, rules, health, employment | Evaluation, biased investment, compensation, development |

Without *労務*, livelihoods are threatened, order is disrupted, and the company’s foundation is shaken. Without talent management, the company cannot win and therefore cannot support workers. The two are interdependent.

> 労務と人材マネジメント、どちらが欠けても「人事」にはなりません。

### Six Constituent Elements of 労務
1. **ルール** (rules) — labor law and *就業規則* (work rules); disciplinary action and harassment countermeasures
2. **労働時間と対価** (working hours and compensation) — hours, holidays, leave, payroll
3. **生活と健康** (life and health) — pregnancy, childcare, nursing care, treatment, disability, occupational safety, mental health, *労災*
4. **雇用** (employment) — joining, transfers, resignation procedures
5. **労使関係** (labor-management relations) — disputes and union response
6. **労務体制** (labor affairs system) — operational/organizational systems and staff development

## Historical Evolution of Japanese Labor

Pre-war (~1941): rigid class system dividing *工員* (blue-collar) from *職員* (white-collar) with large gaps in pay and promotion.

Wartime (1941–1945): labor shortages from conscription; preferential treatment for skilled workers; management began approaching workers.

Post-war (from 1945): GHQ democratization; senior executives purged; workers given participation rights. Labor Union Law enacted December 1945 — Japan’s first modern labor law.

Constitution Article 25: “All people shall have the right to maintain the minimum standards of wholesome and cultured living.” Meaning of “living” evolved:
- Immediate post-war: escaping chaos and poverty
- High-growth (1955–1973): economically and spiritually comfortable life
- Modern (2020~): lifestyles adapted to diversified values and COVID-era change

*労務* itself must evolve with the times.

## Membership-Type vs. Job-Type Employment

**Japan — メンバーシップ型雇用** (membership-type):
- Mass hiring of inexperienced new graduates
- Unlimited duties and work location; company may decide freely
- Employment security expected; long-term tenure, low mobility
- Company-led transfers without consent
- Strict dismissal regulations (abuse of rights if lacking objectively reasonable grounds)

**United States — ジョブ型雇用** (job-type):
- Posts defined first; mid-career or internal posting
- Limited duties; transfers require consent
- Employment at will (unless discriminatory)
- High talent mobility, career autonomy

Reforming only recruitment, grading, evaluation, and pay while leaving work rules, labor contracts, and practice membership-type is insufficient. A foreign securities firm’s restructuring dismissal was held invalid because its work rules contained unlimited-employment language typical of Japanese templates.

> メンバーシップ型とジョブ型は、社会の雇用システム全体を指す広い概念である。

## Three Complementary Roles of Labor Affairs (Holon Structure)

Drawn from Ken Wilber’s Integral Theory. Nested “transcending and including” hierarchy (annual-ring structure). Lower layers are indispensable; without OE, everything collapses.

### OE — Operation Excellence (Foundational)
Process operations smoothly, without delay, and accurately:
- Attendance management, payroll, year-end adjustment
- Join/leave procedures, labor/social insurance, reporting
- Continuous review: standardization, manuals, automation, outsourcing
- Embed new policies into day-to-day processes (overall-optimal, not patchwork)

Many operations directly affect workers’ lives (pensions, childcare-leave benefits, unemployment). View work as “endeavors that support workers’ lives,” not mere tasks.

### RM — Rule Master (Central, Defining Role)
Thoroughly versed in labor laws, *就業規則*, and individual contracts. Catchphrase: always start by checking against work rules (「就業規則に照らす」).

- Answer questions, handle troubles, advise on life events
- Identify the specific article that is the legal basis
- Verify consistency with corporate philosophy and HR policy
- Past response history functions like court precedents (equality and consistency)
- Return to the consulter’s situation and the original intent of the rules; discuss together

Example: nursing-care leave — do not merely quote “93 days”; discuss how to make work and caregiving compatible (telework, short-time work, etc.).

### ST — Strategist
Transformer of the company’s ways of working and rules. Watch macro movements and megatrends; pose questions; design HR measures as “products” using Pain and Gain thinking (focusing on Pains is more effective).

Personnel policies later cited as “other-company examples” originally began as first-time challenges by a courageous ST. Reforming one’s own company can change work styles across society.

**Growth of practitioners**: *深化* (deepen/specialize in a role) or *進化* (evolve from OE → RM → ST). Discernment requires trust between the person and their supervisor, considering organizational structure and personal career.

## Labor Law Framework

There is no single statute named “Labor Law.” It is a collective name. Shared premise: the worker–employer relationship is not equal; workers lack capital and must be protected.

**Public law vs. private law**:
- Public: state supervises (Equal Employment Opportunity Act, Childcare and Family Care Leave Act)
- Private: civil matters, non-intervention (Labor Contract Act — no penal provisions)
- Labor Standards Act has both; violations can lead to arrest/prosecution *and* civil damages

**Legal hierarchy (pyramid)**:
Constitution → Laws (Diet) → Cabinet orders → Ministerial ordinances → Public notices (*告示*) → Circulars (*通達*) → Administrative manuals → Guidelines

Constitution Art. 27: working-condition standards shall be prescribed by law (basis for Labor Standards Act).

Companies must have no gaps in compliance with statutes and public notices. Catch amendment information via:
- Specialized magazines (*労政時報*, *労働新聞*, *ビジネスガイド*)
- MHLW leaflets, Q&A, form templates
- Seminars and expert dispatch
- Council/study-group materials (background and intent — the *Why*)

> 法改正に対応するだけでなく、背景・理由に着目して知識にしていきましょう。

**Essence of law** (two perspectives):
1. From manga *Kingdom*: law is not punishment but a “wish” — the nation’s ideal of how humans should live.
2. Sanskrit *dharma*: that which holds humans so they realize ethics.

Japanese labor law and work rules share this essence.

## Work Rules (就業規則)

*就業規則* = wage, hours, and other conditions uniformly determined per workplace. Possess *法的規範性* (legal normative force): apply regardless of whether workers know or individually agreed.

Three core characteristics:
1. Legal normative force
2. Basis for all judgments in the labor domain
3. Protect the company in court (wording can be the deciding factor)

**Creation duty**: workplaces with 10+ regular workers (including part-time). Create as early as possible even below that — *百害あって一利なし* not to create them. Without them, disciplinary action is generally impossible; recruiting power suffers; responses become ad-hoc and unequal.

**Absolutely mandatory items**: start/end times, rest, holidays, leave; wage determination/calculation/payment; retirement including dismissal grounds.

**Relatively mandatory items**: retirement allowance, worker-borne costs, safety/hygiene, vocational training, disaster compensation, commendation/sanctions, other matters applicable to all workers.

Affiliated regulations (wage rules, childcare-leave rules) are treated as one with the main rules.

**Process for new/revised rules**:
1. Draft
2. Hear opinions from majority union or *過半数代表者* (hearing only — consent not required, but careful dialogue is necessary)
3. File with Labor Standards Inspection Office (two copies, retain stamped copy)
4. Notify workers (distribution, posting, or electronic access)

Relocation requires new filing even if content unchanged.

**Disadvantageous changes** (Labor Contract Act Arts. 9–10): principle is no change without agreement. Exception if (1) made known and (2) reasonable. Reasonableness judged by five interacting elements:
1. Degree of disadvantage
2. Necessity of change
3. Appropriateness of revised content (including compensatory measures)
4. Status of union negotiations
5. Other circumstances

Leading case: Daiyon Bank (1997) — retirement-age extension with wage cut held valid after comprehensive weighing. Write out the five elements in sentences; include external experts.

**Overwrite / minimum-standard effect**: individual contracts below work-rule standards are invalid in that part. Prepare separate work rules per employment type; otherwise regular-employee conditions may overwrite onto all.

**Hierarchy of workplace rules** (different scopes):
法令 (all workers) → 労働協約 (union members) → 労使協定 / 就業規則 (workplace workers) → 個別労働契約 (contracting worker)

*労働協約* and *労使協定* look similar but are legally distinct. Majority representative cannot be a *管理監督者*; selection must be democratic.

**Three Sacred Treasures** of labor personnel: *就業規則*, compiled statute book (*労働関係法規集*, replace yearly), and a long ruler (psychological reassurance from payroll-checking days).

## Working Hours (労働時間)

**Definition**: time the worker is placed under the employer’s command and supervision — including implicit instruction (routine overtime from unmanageable workload).

**Not working hours** (when free use is guaranteed): travel for direct commute/business trips; voluntary training. Reality of how time is spent, not labels, governs.

**Breaks**: ≥45 min if hours >6 and ≤8; ≥1 hour if >8. Must be complete liberation; waiting for phones = working time (*手待時間*). Staggered breaks need a labor-management agreement.

**Statutory hours**: 8/day, 40/week (excluding breaks). *所定労働時間* (prescribed hours) are those in work rules (e.g., 10:00–18:00 with 1-hour break = 7 hours).

**36 Agreement** (Art. 36): required even for one worker if overtime or statutory holiday work will occur. Per-establishment; file, notify, preferably 1-year validity.

- General clause: 45 hours/month, 360 hours/year
- Special clause (temporary unforeseeable spikes only): 720 hours/year; <100 hours any month including holiday work; average ≤80 hours over 2–6 month periods; months >45 hours limited to 6/year

**Grasping hours** (MHLW guidelines): employer duty to confirm start/end times. Prefer objective records (time cards, IC logs, PC usage). Self-declaration only if unavoidable — with explanation, actual-condition surveys, no hindrance of reporting, no artificial caps. Calculate in 1-minute units.

**管理監督者** (true supervisory managers — high hurdle, judged by actual conditions, not title):
Four elements: important job content; responsibility/authority; working style incompatible with ordinary regulation; commensurate treatment. Ratio ≥10% of workforce tends to be viewed as excessive.

Exempt only from hours/rest/holiday provisions. Still subject to: late-night premium, annual paid leave (including 5-day specification duty), physician interview if >80 hours overtime, and working-hour tracking for health/safety-care duty.

**Flextime**: most realistic flexible system. Fix total hours for a settlement period (normally 1 month, max 3). Optional core time. Premium still owed if actual hours exceed statutory total, for late-night, and for statutory holiday work (excluded from flextime).

Statutory totals for 1-month settlement: 31-day 177.1h; 30-day 171.4h; 29-day 165.7h; 28-day 160.0h.

**Side-job hour aggregation**: statutory 8/40, multi-month 80-hour average, and <100-hour monthly limits apply across employment contracts. MHLW *管理モデル* lets each workplace set its own caps so the sum stays under limits; side-job employer pays premium on all its hours; no continuous cross-employer tracking. Stipulate in work rules.

**Holidays vs. leave**: *休日* = no labor obligation; *休暇* = exemption from a scheduled workday (does not count toward holiday totals). At least one *法定休日* per week (or 4 in 4 weeks). Create an annual calendar distinguishing statutory holidays, prescribed holidays, and workdays.

Hourly rate for monthly-salary premium pay:
```
(annual prescribed workdays × hours per day) ÷ 12 = average monthly prescribed hours
monthly salary ÷ that figure = hourly rate
```
Round <50 sen down, ≥50 sen up.

**振替休日 vs. 代休**:
- *振替休日*: advance swap; if valid, no holiday premium (but overtime premium if week >40). Consecutive work max 12 days. Record in advance; preferably within same wage period.
- *代休*: rest after holiday work already occurred; holiday premium still owed; 36 Agreement required for statutory holiday work. Treating actual *代休* as *振替休日* to avoid premium is illegal.

**Annual paid leave**: after 6 months continuous service + 80% attendance. Days: 10 → 11 → 12 → 14 → 16 → 18 → 20 (by tenure). Proportional for part-timers. Employer must grant on requested date except when it interferes with normal operations (high hurdle). Duty to specify timing of 5 days for anyone granted ≥10 days (including managers). Create *年休管理簿*, retain 3 years. Penalty: fine ≤300,000 yen per person. Unify base date in practice.

## Compensation and Payroll

**Five Principles of Wage Payment** (Labor Standards Act Art. 24): currency, direct to worker, in full, ≥once a month, on a fixed date. Bank transfer needs worker’s own-name consent. Non-statutory deductions need a 24 Agreement.

**Minimum wage**: regional + industry-specific; check yearly (~October). Exclude overtime and bonuses; exclude fixed-overtime portion when checking. Convert monthly pay to hourly equivalent.

**Payroll flow**:
1. Attendance aggregation (critical: system initial settings — rounding, flextime, weekly 40-hour overtime)
2. Payment-amount calculation (basic, allowances, premiums)
3. Deduction-amount calculation (employment insurance, social insurance, income tax, resident tax)

> 勤怠を正しく集計することが給与計算のすべての起点です。

**Premium-wage base**: exhaustive exclusion list only — family, commuting, separation, children’s education, housing (substance, not name), temporary wages, wages for periods >1 month. Uniform “family” or “housing” allowances often cannot be excluded.

Rates: overtime 25% (50% beyond 60 hours/month); holiday 35%; night 25% (additive — night overtime = 75%). Statutory holiday hours excluded from 60-hour count; prescribed-holiday hours included. Managers still get night premium.

**Fixed overtime pay** (*固定残業代*): valid only with *対価性* (expressly stated as overtime consideration in rules/contracts) and *明確区分性* (separately itemized on notices and slips). Still calculate actual overtime and pay the difference. >80 hours has been held contrary to public order.
