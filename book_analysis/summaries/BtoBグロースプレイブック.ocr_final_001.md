## Historical Evolution of Japanese B2B / SaaS (~20 Years)

B2B evolved with technology and DX. From the 2010s, cloud SaaS became central—not just IT, but a mechanism improving operations and ways of working, extending into sales, marketing, CX, and decision-making.

### Phase timeline
- **Late 2000s–early 2010s (dawn):** Cloud shift from “own” to “use.” Early SMB SaaS: Rakus, Sansan, Cybozu (attendance, expenses, business cards). SMEs without IT staff digitized cheaply.
- **Mid–late 2010s (DX center):** SmartHR, Money Forward, freee cloudified paper-and-*hanko* back office. Salesforce’s “No Software” popularized SaaS globally; HubSpot/Zendesk standardized ARR, churn, NRR. Core ideas: subscription *stock* revenue, Customer Success, and Salesforce’s *The Model* (marketing–sales–CS coordination). Highly reproducible and investor-friendly.
- **2018–2021 (SaaS bubble):** Even firms with a few hundred million yen ARR got tens-to-hundreds of billions in market cap. COVID/remote work accelerated cloud migration.
- **After 2022:** Sharp valuation correction. Growth slowed more than expected; being valued only on growth rate ended.

### Japan-specific evolution (not a US copy)
- **Phase 1 — SME operational efficiency:** Largest Japanese SaaS market (Rakus 楽楽精算, freee, SmartHR). Penetration lagged due to IT-literacy gaps, especially regional firms. Niche products often stalled. Self-service assumption failed; heavy CS/onboarding became a personnel-cost burden.
- **Phase 2 — Enterprise expansion:** Higher unit prices. Playd (CX data) passed ¥10B ARR. Standardization/non-customization often failed against legacy systems; implementation-support costs ballooned with scale.
- **Phase 3 — “SaaS mediated by people”:** CS, consulting, and *BPaaS* (SaaS + BPO). SMEs outsource entire processes; enterprises get hybrid product × consulting. Classic product-only scale does not fit Japan; a distinctive ecosystem fused people and systems.

> US logic: “if people use it, it spreads.” Japanese logic: “using it is only the start of the real introduction process.” PLG is hard. Ringisho, multi-department consensus, SIer/consultant gatekeepers, annual/multi-year contracts, and audit needs make copied KPIs and *The Model* specialized orgs inefficient in a smaller market.

Japan’s SaaS market ~¥1.4 trillion (2023) vs North America ~¥24 trillion (~1/17). Market-size difference is an unavoidable premise. Composite hurdles: market size, complex adoption, SIer dependency, IT-literacy gaps (Chatwork 2023: only 32.5% of SME managers using SaaS), thin early-adopter layer (cautious “confirm other cases first” culture).

## The Five Walls (Trials) from PMF to ¥10 Billion

The *Growth Map* (Figure 1-1) frames five successive, Japan-specific walls. Identify which wall is approaching and prepare accordingly. Not every firm must go overseas, but some redefine the market abroad as domestic limits appear.

1. **幻のPMF** (*Phantom / illusory PMF*)
2. **拡大の谷** (*Valley of Expansion*)
3. **キャズムの壁** (*Chasm Wall*)
4. **マルチプロダクトの沼** (*Multi-product swamp*)
5. **市場の限界と覇権争い** (*Market limits and struggle for hegemony*)

### 1. Phantom PMF (幻のPMF)

Founder feels the business is taking shape: sales appear, fundraising conversations sound promising. Revenue often depends on the founder’s network, passion, and “the president explained it with enthusiasm”—*Product + CEO*, not product power. Figure 2-1 (President’s Added Value Graph): CEO overlay lifts perceived value over the customer’s purchase line; regular salespeople often cannot.

**Danger:** When the founder leaves the front line, numbers stall. Superficial revenue and low churn mislead (relationship-driven unused contracts). Labor-intensive founder consulting inflates unit prices while killing scalability.

**True-PMF diagnostics (three lenses):**
- **Usage reality**, not churn: frequency, feature utilization, satisfaction. Dialogue and observation refine real value.
- **Escape CEO dependency:** revenue from ads, seminars, outbound, partners—leads the CEO is not involved in.
- **Sales reproducibility:** people other than the CEO can sell. Hiring a Keyence-style top closer fails without founder-built stories, scenarios, and mechanisms. Early-stage needs *business-development sales* (define value from zero), not maximizing an existing system.

Do not postpone polishing *価値の定義と磨き込み*. True PMF assumes **no CEO added value**. After PMF, verify dual engines from *The Lean Startup*:
- *Value hypothesis:* does the product truly solve a problem and become indispensable?
- *Growth hypothesis* = GTM: will it spread? “If you make something good it will spread” is an illusion.

### GTM Strategy: Who × What × How (before and after first PMF)

GTM is the key between PMF and scale—rarely disclosed publicly. First step: concretely depict the **future revenue mix** that produces the target (e.g. 100 customers at ¥10M ARR vs 2,000 at ¥500k). Mix dictates org form (irreversible hiring/culture).

- ~2,000 accounts → volume *The Model*: IS / FS / CS specialization, large headcount.
- ~100 deep accounts → ABM, small elite team. Copying *The Model* fails.
- Culture: startups/IT (casual) vs large enterprise (suits, *-sama*, experienced talent). Wrong culture early creates lasting drag (Knowledge Work Inc. designed an “adult organization” from day one).

**Who × What first, How later.** Same product needs different value propositions by segment (chatbot: conversion vs call-center reduction vs fewer EC errors; SFA: Excel escape vs nationwide analysis vs IS handoff). Plot segments on attractiveness vs difficulty (LTV vs volume). Sequence: beachhead via easier adjacent segment (RevComm: staffing before real estate; UPSIDER: IPO-bound startups then ad agencies), then change *How* (aerial warfare → muddy door-to-door). Three expansion axes: industry (vertical), company size (horizontal), area (urban vs regional). Embed sequence in the business plan; execute, verify, revise.

### 2. Valley of Expansion (拡大の谷)

Typically after true PMF, around several hundred million to ~¥1 billion / ARR ¥1億. First market exhausts faster than expected; new acquisition slows; churn/contraction compound. False security from case studies and headcount. Guerrilla management still works to ARR ¥1億; beyond that, *仕組み化* is mandatory—but **not too early** (freeze a non-working state).

**Three causes:** Japanese thin early-adopter layer (quick saturation); shift in customer segment (2nd PMF needed); *属人性* (person-dependency) in sales and CS.

**2nd PMF:** re-fit Who × What × How for the next (non-early-adopter) layer—between first PMF and the chasm. Verbalize *What* from win/loss and successful *use cases* (not just signed logos). Document driver conditions, redesign personas (buyer/user/champion), make **non-targets explicit**, run short verification cycles. Value *translation* by segment; product updates on a clear thread.

**How (acquisition):** inbound saturates; shift to attacking sales. Optimal channel depends on Who and LTV (guideline LTV:CAC ~5x). High LTV expands tactics (advisors, exhibitions, regional banks, seminars). Early: one-point concentration on one efficient channel (~3 months verification). Stand up BDR in-house before outsourcing. Then cross-channel CX as one story (exhibition → white paper → MA scoring → BDR).

**Three branching points of growers vs stallers:** (1) Who/What redefinition and 2nd PMF, (2) waiting → attacking sales, (3) eliminate individual dependency.

**Sales systematization:** diagnose growth model via **Marketing Sales 12MAP** (product type × customer type). Product axis: existing-business replacement → new value on existing business → new-category creation (education cost rises). Customer axis: individuals / ventures / enterprises / traditional SMBs. Example: 1-B (replacement × venture) → *The Model*; 2-C (new value × enterprise) → ABM / Challenger Sales. Companies must **move squares** as they scale (SmartHR: 1-B web → 2-C enterprise org).

Standardize: hearing items, demo scenarios, proposal structure, competitor talk, closing, internal-approval (*上申*) formats. Visualize stages, conversion, cycle, win/loss reasons. Onboarding curriculum (5 steps) shortens time-to-productivity. Convert tacit knowledge to explicit so headcount growth produces revenue growth.

**Customer Success as growth engine, not helpdesk:** churn sets long-term ceiling (~10% or below to keep growing 20 years). Standardize first-90-days onboarding. Mechanize upsell/cross-sell with maps, health scores (quantitative + qualitative), NRR ≥ 100%. CS owns expansion after 定着.

**Partner sales:** not “more agents = more capacity” (most agents do not move). Success = affinity on customer structure, sales motion, and incentives (*reason to sell* for field reps, not executives). Design 2–3 years: Design (0–6m playbook) → Verification (joint toss-up) → Expansion (independent operation, 20–30% of sources). Enablement, KPI dashboards, success-model sharing. Examples: Money Forward (zeirishi network + familiar “legacy” UI); Safie (plug-in-power simplicity).

Self-check: Is growth already a **reproducible structure**? Crossing the valley is the foothold for ARR ¥10億 and the chasm.

### 3. Chasm Wall (キャズムの壁)

Often ARR ¥1–2 billion. Gap from early adopters to early majority (Rogers). In Japan it appears **earlier and thicker**: weak try-first culture, multi-layer approvals, PoC止まり, SIer/IT/audit/legal, uneven literacy, smaller SMB TAM forcing earlier enterprise.

Strategic fork: deepen SMB (long accumulation) vs enter enterprise vs dual engines. **SMB strengths:** speed, short cycles, inbound, efficiency outcomes. Rakus/SMS show SMB concentration can still build large companies. **SMB limits:** low ACV, high churn, price sensitivity, heavy CS vs thin revenue, flow-dependent ARR. Plateau ~¥1–2B is common.

**Enterprise is a different world.** High unit prices (tens–hundreds of millions ARR) vs 6–12+ month cycles, 5.4+ stakeholders, RFPs, procurement cost pressure, non-functional requirements (security, audit, SSO, APIs). SMB: customers change ops to fit product. Japanese enterprise: fit existing work first. Company-vs-company protocol (executives to 部長+). Professional services and high-touch onboarding are growth drivers, not cost centers—buyers pay for “not failing.”

**ABC / IABC sales:** Actor (president/top sales, short high-leverage interventions) vs Bridge (relationship, scenario, follow-up). Maximize scarce Actor time. Executive-to-executive trust. Design PoCs with purpose, pass/fail, limited stakeholders, conversion terms—or fall into *PoC hell*.

**Enterprise CS = per-company project management:** WBS, change-management, 90-day activation, staged pilots, Gantt with tech + PS. Monetize PS (requirements, migration, training). Measure business KPIs (labor saved, errors, NPS), not just feature usage. Compound interest from existing enterprise accounts, not endless new PoCs.

**Rebuild partner sales for enterprise:** joint proposals/projects (SIers, installed base), expertise/reliability over channel breadth, symbol customers, role-cutting for speed gap, dedicated alliance managers. Partners as a *community for market conquest*, not a channel.

Crossing requires near-restructuring: dedicated enterprise org, non-functional readiness, PS, rebuilt partners. Postponing at the ¥2B wall typically prevents crossing.
